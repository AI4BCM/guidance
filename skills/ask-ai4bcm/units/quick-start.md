# Quick Start

Using artificial intelligence in business continuity management

For BCM professionals, their managers and the colleagues who work beside them.

## AI prepares, people decide

Ask AI to prepare, compare, summarise, retrieve, and challenge. But require people to decide, approve, and act.

- Use only approved tools, never free public ones.
- Base every answer on approved sources.
- Have a person review output before anyone relies on it.
- Let only people approve, declare, invoke, accept risk or speak externally.
- Keep fallback methods for essential BCM work.
Start with:

- drafting non-sensitive content
- summarising approved material
- preparing BIA interviews
- checking that plans agree
- extracting actions from debriefs
Plug this guidance into the AI tool you already use: ai4bcm.org/plug-it-in

Minimum control checklist

## Check these eight points before every AI task

- the tool is approved for the type of data involved
- the information is suitable for that environment
- the task is narrow and clear
- the output is based on approved, current sources, citations checked
- the reviewer knows the subject, has the sources and the time, and knows who to pass it to if unsure
- any actions or record changes have approval gates
- you can show afterwards who did what, and from which sources
- fallback methods exist if the tool is unavailable
The more sensitive the data, the tighter the control: BIA, crisis, personal, supplier, recovery and security detail, board papers, open audit findings.

## Some decisions never rest on AI alone

- formal approval or sign-off
- autonomous declaration of incidents or crises
- autonomous invocation of continuity plans
- unsupervised external communications
- statements to a regulator, auditor or certification body
- automatic risk acceptance
- uncontrolled amendment of BCM or BCMS records
Never acceptable, whoever approves it

- making up evidence, audit material or records
- replacing human welfare, ethical or safety judgement
- entering passwords, keys or access tokens into any tool
## Start small, then grow one level at a time

A good first task passes four tests:

- Do you do it at least monthly?
- Would you rather not do it?
- Could a new colleague learn its steps in minutes?
- Can a person approve it, and work go on without the tool?
Level 1 · Ad hoc.

Start with low-risk tasks.

Level 2 · Repeatable.

Share tested prompts and review rules.

Level 3 · Defined.

Connect approved repositories once governance is ready.

Level 4 · Measured.

Test, and count quality and failures, before going live.

Level 5 · Optimising.

Extend on measured evidence, once governance is mature.

Levels 3 to 5 change systems and governance: there, experience counts.
