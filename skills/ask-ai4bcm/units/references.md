## Further reading

- **ISACA** (2023). *CMMI V3.0*, a licensed model; the level names were read on the CMMI Institute page. Read it for the ladder that levels 1 to 3 of this guidance follow. The name Measured and every description are AI4BCM’s own; level 5 takes CMMI’s name, Optimising. <https://cmmiinstitute.com/learning/appraisals/levels>
- **Kalai, A. T., Nachum, O., Vempala, S. S. and Zhang, E.** (2025). *Why Language Models Hallucinate*. OpenAI. Made-up answers tend to be confident and specific. <https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/d04913be-3f6f-4d2b-b283-ff432ef4aaa5/why-language-models-hallucinate.pdf>
- **NCSC** (2026). *Managing the cyber risk of agentic AI*, blog post, 20 August 2026, interim advice that formal guidance will replace. Read it when an agent, rather than a workflow, is proposed. Controls matched to the level of autonomy, sandboxing, logs that cannot be changed, and the ability to stop it at once. <https://ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/managing-the-cyber-risk-of-agentic-ai>
- **NIST** (2024). *AI RMF Generative AI Profile*, NIST AI 600-1, July 2024, companion to AI RMF 1.0, which NIST says is being revised. MS-2.5-003: check sources and citations in generated output. GV-6.2-006: fallback may include doing things manually. Workflow design in the online copy. <https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1>
- **NIST**. *AI RMF Playbook*, live companion to AI RMF 1.0, carrying the banner “The AI RMF 1.0 is being updated.” A list of actions on third-party dependency, shutting down with backup systems and decommissioning. No maturity levels. <https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook>
- **OWASP** (2025). *AI Maturity Assessment (AIMA)*, Version 1.0, 11 August 2025 (Excel toolkit v1.0.1). Read it when a claimed capability needs evidence. Its detailed assessment checks evidence, “not just ‘paper compliance’”. The released PDF defines eight domains, 24 practices, two streams and three levels per practice. <https://owasp.org/www-project-ai-maturity-assessment>

## Standards this guidance works alongside

- **AICPA and CIMA**. SOC 1 and SOC 2 reports, with the *2017 Trust Services Criteria (With Revised Points of Focus — 2022)* behind a SOC 2. When a provider answers one of the tool checks with an assurance report, and you need to see what it covered and over which period. <https://aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022>
- **COSO**. *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (2013), with its 2023 supplemental guidance on internal control over sustainability reporting and its 2026 guidance, *Achieving Effective Internal Control Over Generative AI*. When internal control, rather than IT, asks where an AI-supported step sits in a process. <https://coso.org/guidance-on-ic>
- **ISACA**. *COBIT 2019*. When AI use in BCM has to be reported inside an IT governance framework, and you want that framework’s own words for it. <https://isaca.org/resources/cobit>
- **ISO 22301:2019**. *Security and resilience — Business continuity management systems — Requirements*, second edition, with Amendment 1:2024. The RTO and BCMS definitions; cited on p. 13 and in the glossary. <https://www.iso.org/standard/75106.html>
- **ISO 31000:2018**. *Risk management — Guidelines*, second edition. When a BIA result has to travel into the organisation’s wider risk work, and you want the vocabulary that work already uses. <https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html>
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**. *Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Information security management systems — Requirements*, third edition, with Amendment 1:2024. When the approval you need for a tool or a connector sits with information security. <https://www.iso.org/standard/82875.html>
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023**. *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system*, first edition. When the organisation wants a management system for its AI use rather than a rule for one task. The newest ISO standard here, and the only one written for AI. <https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html>
- **NIST** (2024). *The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0*, NIST CSWP 29, 26 February 2024. Plain words for cyber outcomes, for a scenario, an exercise or a conversation with IT. Free to read, and the only standard here the online copy carries. <https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29>
- **PCI Security Standards Council**. *PCI Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)*, v4.0.1. When the material in front of you is payment card data, which the data rules do not list as a class of its own. Classify it before you enter it. [pcisecuritystandards.org/document\_library](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/)
