# Will AI replace BCM managers?

When tasks become cheaper, people often do more of them, for as long as demand keeps growing (Bessen, 2018). The increased efficiency of steam engines led to greater coal consumption (Jevons, 1865). Similarly, when writing reports becomes cheaper, more BIAs, refreshers and exercises are requested. The role of the BCM will change and grow for as long as there is a significant amount of BCM work that AI cannot do.

## Purpose

**Purpose** Ask AI to prepare, compare, summarise, retrieve, and challenge information.

Who this guidance is for

This guidance is for BCM professionals, for managers and governance owners who oversee BCM
work, and for the people in resilience, risk, crisis management, IT, security, facilities and
operations who work beside BCM teams. It follows common BCM lifecycle activities, from
governance and embedding to analysis, design, implementation and validation. These are generic
on purpose, so the guidance works with any BCM framework, a formal management system or an
internal method.

## Introduction

AI assists BCM managers with written tasks at each of the six stages. For instance, during the 'Govern and Embed' stages, it drafts policy wording from your approved framework for review and adapts awareness messages for each audience. During the Analyse and Design stages, it prepares business impact analysis (BIA) interview guides and questions the assumptions behind the strategies. During the implementation and validation stages, it drafts plans, writes exercise scenarios and categorises debriefing notes into actions.

## The brain in a jar

Imagine the language model (LLM) is a brain in a jar. On its own, it can only read what you paste into the chat box and generate a response. Its usefulness depends entirely on the questions you ask and the context you provide. What you connect it to determines its capabilities. For example, if you connect it to your email or calendar, it can scan your inbox, draft replies independently or update a record.
