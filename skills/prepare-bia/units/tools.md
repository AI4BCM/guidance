# Choosing a tool

## Which tool for which task

The table below provides an overview of the five service types, the tasks they are best suited for, and whether they train on your content.

| Service | What for | Typical tasks | Trains on your content? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free public service | Generic brainstorming only, no confidential content | Fictional scenario ideas; nothing about your organisation | It can. Check the privacy setting; never enter BCM data |
| Individual paid service | Low-risk individual productivity, if formally approved | Learning BCM terms and methods; public threat identification and regulatory research | Depends on the plan and its setting; check before use |
| Team or business subscription | Basic internal use if approved and governed | The same, once approved and governed | Not by default, by the vendor's own statement; confirm in your contract |
| Enterprise or corporate licence | Often right for live BCM work | Policy and reporting, BIA, plans, exercises, incident support; with logging: scheduled reminders and reviews | Not by default, by the vendor's own statement; confirm in your contract |
| Private, self-hosted or on-premise | Highly sensitive or regulated use cases | The most sensitive BIA, options and plans | Your own set-up decides |

Be aware that even tools of the same type can keep your data for different lengths of time, offer different contract terms, and give connectors different access. Therefore, before granting a tool access to your data, complete the seven checks on the next page.

## Before you approve a tool

Before an AI tool can access your data, it must pass the seven checks below.

- how long the provider keeps your data, how it is deleted, where it is stored if that matters, and whether the provider trains on it
- privacy and confidentiality terms in the contract, and licence terms for what you upload, including your own licences for standards and copyrighted material
- sign-in through your identity system, access by role, what each connector may reach, and which sources the tool may use
- audit logging and reporting
- whether it suits sensitive operational content and meets your AI policy and information classification rules
- a fallback if the tool is unavailable, an exit plan if the provider fails, the provider’s breach notification terms, and whether your third-party risk process and your incident plan cover the provider
- a date to check these answers again, at each renewal and whenever the provider changes the model

An enterprise label does not inform you about the provider's retention policy and administrative settings, which determine which data is stored and where. When using a chat app, for instance, the vendor has the ability to switch or retire the model without providing prior notice. Therefore, it is recommended to set a fixed date for a re-check. Most of this list matches ETSI EN 304 223 (2025), the UK Government AI Playbook (2025), the NCSC and CISA guidelines (2023), and the SEI model (2026).

## Categories

- **Generative AI assistants** — drafting, summarising, comparing, rewriting.
- **Retrieval-based search and knowledge tools** — answers based on approved repositories: BCMS documents, checks that plans agree, staff questions.
- **Analytical AI and machine learning** — dependency mapping, clustering exercise issues, trend and concentration analysis.
- **Meeting capture and transcription** — BIA interviews, debriefs, actions.
- **Workflow and automation** — triggers, routing, approvals: review reminders, plan review when something changes, evidence pack assembly.
- **BI and reporting with AI features** — narrative from BCMS data for management review.
- **BCM and resilience platforms with AI features** — structured records, plans, exercises, action tracking.
- **External risk intelligence** — horizon scanning and supply chain monitoring.
- **Collaboration and learning content tools with AI features** — structured review, workshop design, awareness and training material.
- **Incident notification and communication tools** — staff notification, escalation routing and communications drafts; sending stays supervised.
- **Private or controlled model hosting** — governed access inside your own environment.

## Where a tool can run

| Where it runs | Main caution |
|---|---|
| Free public service | Never use it for BCM data, internal records, incident or licensed material |
| Individual paid service | Better features do not mean acceptable privacy, governance or licensing |
| Team or business subscription | Do due diligence on access control, retention, administration and contract |
| Enterprise or corporate licence | It depends on configuration, retention, identity integration, connector control and contract terms. The licence covers the platform, never the material you upload |
| Private, self-hosted or on-premise | It needs technical skill, mature governance and operational support |
