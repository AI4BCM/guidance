# Five principles

**At every AI maturity level and in every stage of your BCM lifecycle, these five principles apply:**

## 1. Know AI and its limits

Anyone using AI for BCM work is familiar with its strengths and weaknesses. It can invent facts and sources, overlook information that is not included in its sources, and appear confident when it is incorrect. Your organisation should help those using AI to understand these limitations (European Union, 2024, Article 4, as amended in 2026).

## 2. Match the tool to the classification

Classify the material before you paste it into a tool. The more sensitive it is, and the more serious an error would be, the more controlled the tool must be. Free public tools never get BCM data. The classification levels are listed under *Data rules*, p. 7.

## 3. Checking AI output and approving what it does

AI-generated output should be reviewed by a competent person before it is included in a BCMS document, report or decision. This person must have the necessary resources and time to do so.

AI can only act independently within a predefined environment owned by a specific individual, with permissions set in the tool itself and every action logged. It must always be possible to stop AI.

Each organisation determines which actions require approval on a case-by-case basis. The more mature your company is, and the more guardrails you have in place throughout your BCM lifecycle, the more you can hand over to AI. It is worth tracking the number of approvals and the rate at which AI output is overridden, but these alone do not demonstrate effective review. A very low override rate can also mean that nobody checks any more (IMDA, 2026, section 2.2.2).

## 4. AI answers only from approved sources, and cites them

Link your AI tool to approved internal repositories and official external sources only. Ensure that it only provides answers from these sources. Regularly check the cited passage against the claim it supports.

## 5. Quality over impressing

A long, polished document is not necessarily a good one. AI can make things look finished quickly. Read everything before sending it out, and only keep what will be used and makes sense.

## Minimum control checklist

Before any AI-assisted BCM task, check that:

- the tool is approved for the type of data involved
- the information is suitable for that environment
- the output is based on approved, current sources, citations checked
- reviewer has the necessary knowledge, access to the relevant sources, and time to check
- any actions or record changes have approval gates
- you can identify afterwards who did what, and from which sources
- fallback methods exist if the tool is unavailable
